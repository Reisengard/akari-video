Status: draft. The coordinator and the owner have not ruled.

## 2. Comparing the proposals (3 proposals by 8 views)

A is display-only separation. B separates real data, with a link, from the moment a new cut is inserted. C keeps embedded audio as the default and converts to B's shape with Split audio. For B and C, `audio: false` is a proposed addition **directly on the visual media item**, and `link: <cutId>` is a proposed addition **directly on the audio item**. They are not current fields. C's Split audio creates the audio item. The following Unlink deletes the relation. They are different operations.

**Shared proposed vocabulary and invariants for B and C.** As an additive change on `version: 2`, add `MediaItemV2.audio?: false` (omitted means supply the embedded audio as today) and `AudioMediaItemV2.link?: string`. Do not allow `audio` unconditionally on the whole shared ItemV2Base. Limit it to visual media. `link` is an edit-time relation to a lasting cut id in the same edit. It is not an instruction to recompute the audio source or `at` from the link target at render time. The first version is one to one. The audio item owns its own src, in, out, at, and duration.

So that dialogue is not misclassified as Sound effects after a split, the proposal adds `speech` to audio role. speech means "a ducking key". The lane and the owning track mean "the mute target". That separates today's coupling "if kind is speech, mute cuts". Substituting narration can change the narration-or-transcript keys and the auto-level defaults. role speech remains after unlink. Extend the current sfx, narration, and bgm enumeration and the legacy view explicitly. Do not silently drop an unknown role to sfx.

The proposal also adds `AudioMediaItemV2.mute?: boolean` (omitted means false). The source cut's source.mute moves to this item mute. The source visual track's muted is carried onto the dedicated destination audio track's muted. Do not use -60 dB as a stand-in for silence. After the split, audio is audible only when the audio item is not mute and the owning audio track is not muted. The source visual track's muted does not affect the real audio. On an explicit split, the source cut's source.gain_db and mute move and are then deleted. If audio:false and a leftover source gain are both present by hand, lint reports the gain as a declaration that has no effect.

A split, in one edit transaction, allocates a new audio id, creates the audio item, sets cut.audio to false, and sets the link. The footage file is not copied. The same `sources[].id` is used. If the pre-write conflict or lock check fails, the whole transaction is cancelled. Unlink deletes only `link`. source, time, gain, and audio:false stay. A normal linked delete removes both items. Deleting only the audio with Alt leaves cut.audio false. Deleting only the picture removes the surviving audio's link, so no dangling reference remains. "Already split" does not depend only on the link existing, so a later split after unlink does not duplicate the audio.

lint requires audio:false on the target of an audio item that has a link. It does not require the reverse. A picture that only has audio:false, and role speech audio with no link, are valid states after a delete or an unlink. Reusing the same `sources[].id` is not by itself double supply. Check the linked pair's embedded-enabled state and the ids that are actually supplied.

## 3. Coordinator recommendation and why

**The recommendation is C (explicit split) as the real data model, plus A's compatibility derived row for an unsplit cut.** This is a proposal to the coordinator. It is not a ruling. C alone, if it only removes the speaker from the picture row, loses the audio entry for existing projects, so the first introduction includes the compatibility derived row. A alone cannot move audio by itself or hold volume keyframes. Making B the default forces an unverified general conversion onto every insert.

Move toward the goal in this order.

1. Add the vocabulary to the schema, the reader, and lint, and keep the meaning and the bytes of old documents. Do not publish split writes before supply support exists. At the edit-store read boundary used for execution, a handwritten new vocabulary is an unsupported-feature error, so it does not flow into the old execution path. Keep that separate from a structural read used for validation.
2. Introduce the same ownership rule on the four paths and on preview-server. The embedded side supplies zero times when audio is false. The independent side supplies once. The picture item stays on the timeline, in the Captions projection, and in the composite. Split speech plays at the audio item's time on the owning track, and the ducking key is projected to that output position. After unlink, or after moving only the audio, do not also adopt the original cut position as a speech key.
3. Ship the opt-in Split audio command and link editing. The first targets are limited to a **top-level cut with no anchor, no nesting, speed omitted or 1, no freeze, and no transition that crossfades audio before or after**, and only when the footage is confirmed to have audio. duration, including frame rounding, agrees with the footage interval. A still, or silent footage, is out of scope. An undetected case holds the write until a result exists. An unsupported target keeps playing and Exporting as it is. It is not silently converted by approximation.
4. For each visual track, put a derived row such as "Program audio (V1)" into the audio group, and put the unsplit cut's waveform and speaker there. The id is derived stably from the source track id, and it is distinct from the existing BGM `t-audio-implied`. The derived row's speaker writes visual track.muted. The real audio row's speaker writes audio track.muted. A split portion drops the derived chip and is drawn only on the real audio row. Last, picture headers become eye plus lock, Captions become eye plus lock, and audio becomes a speaker. M/S and a mixer come later.

A derived row is not saved in edit.json tracks, and it does not change z order. The split destination is a dedicated real audio track per source visual track, created when needed, and it does not change muted on existing BGM or Sound effects. If a previous receiver's muted differs from the source visual track, prepare another row with the same mute state. Do not overwrite existing audio state. When a derived row becomes empty, remove only that display.

A normal move while linked adds the same integer frame delta to `at` on both sides. A trim computes the boundary from each side's footage range and speed, and applies atomically only a delta that is valid on both sides. Shifting one side with Alt keeps the link. A later normal move preserves the current sync offset. A reload does not realign the times. Inside the first supported range, the sync offset can be shown from `at` and source.in. A future speed change is refused until both sides can change together.

Picture keyframes such as transform stay on the picture. Volume keyframes use local integer frames inside the audio item as the source of truth. A move leaves `t` unchanged. A head trim shifts `t` by the boundary delta and drops points outside the range. The new boundary value is filled by the existing interpolation rule. When the minimum point count is not met, fold explicitly to an equivalent constant gain. Do not copy one side's keyframe edit onto the other. If any item or track being changed is locked, a normal link operation refuses the whole change. An Alt operation does not bypass the lock on the side that actually changes. Undo and redo restore the pair and the key changes as one unit.

**Compatibility range.** An existing edit.json that is not split is not rewritten by open, display, playback, Export, or any processing that does not use the new feature. Existing canonical serialization fixtures return the same bytes from the old and new serializers. An explicit normalize of non-canonical whitespace or key order is not required to match the original text, but filling default keys because of this vocabulary, and automatic splitting, are forbidden. The existing v0 and v1 migration procedure does not change. This ticket does not promise wider reading of old formats the current reader cannot read. An explicit split edits only the target cut and the receiver. It does not change unrelated items, the content or order of sources, captions.json, motion files, or footage. Opening a split file in an old app is a separate compatibility condition.

Ticket 1 requires L0. Tickets 2 to 5 require L0 plus L1 for the execution paths that ticket touches. Cross-path acceptance compares supply count and span gain on test footage with master processing off. The first proposed acceptance values are a time boundary within 1 output frame, and an RMS difference within 0.1 dB on the same span. Double supply is also detected by how many times a declared id is listed, not only by the span measurement. Do not call an existing DSP or codec difference "bit identical". When the master is on, the existing audio QC conditions also apply. A threshold adjustment is written into the implementation ticket, with the measurement as the ground.

A general conversion for freeze, transition, speed change, and nesting or anchor is outside the five tickets. Until it exists, the explicit split command refuses those targets. A freeze's silence span and the audio before and after it in particular need several audio items or an extra time map, and the one-to-one link has to be extended first. Do not widen the target by simply copying in, out, and duration.

## 5. Open points (owner ruling, at most 5)

- Is it acceptable to ship C plus the compatibility derived row first, and switch the default for a new insert to B after measurement?
- May the first Split audio be limited to the simple cut in section 3? In what order do freeze, transition, speed change, and nesting become later general conversions?
- Adopt the added speech role, the audio item mute, the one-to-one link, keeping the sync offset across Alt, and the rule that deleting audio does not revive the embedded audio?
- Is the derived row named "Program audio (V1)", with a dedicated real-audio receiver per source visual track? How is the entry for order and rename shown?
- While automatic migration stays off, offer a later opt-in "split the selected cuts together"? Is a bulk conversion of an existing project only after the individual command has been accepted?
